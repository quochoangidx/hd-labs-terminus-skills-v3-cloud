Our inventory controller has been checking the variance reports that the counting package in `/app/src/cyclecount` produces against the distribution centre's cycle count procedure, `/app/docs/cycle-count-procedure.md` (CC-2), and they do not agree. Recounts the team walked back to take are ignored, adjusted lines post the wrong number of units to stock, A-class pallets two units short sail through as "ok", lines that should be sent back for a recount are marked for adjustment, and the shrink figure comes out below nought whenever a surplus turns up on the same sheet. The procedure is the authority for this report. Change the package so that the report printed by `python3 /app/tools/cyclecount_run.py SHEET.json` follows it. Each of its rules holds for every count sheet within the limits of its rule 1.3, not only for sheets like the one in `/app/examples/`. Sheets will be reported by running our own copy of `/app/tools/cyclecount_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the procedure governs. Where the procedure gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the procedure does define, and every other step still follows the procedure. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the procedure does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: quantities, costs, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.3, two lines with the same SKU, and files that do not follow the README.

Sheets of one to three hundred lines will be reported. They include every class letter, system quantities from nought to 100,000 units, counts and recounts above, below and equal to the system quantity, recounts that bring a line back inside its tolerance and ones that do not, variances that land exactly on a tolerance and one unit past it, tolerances that fall on a fraction of a unit, and sheets with shortages and surpluses together. Every key the README lists is compared exactly, as a JSON integer or string, and so is the order of the lines.
