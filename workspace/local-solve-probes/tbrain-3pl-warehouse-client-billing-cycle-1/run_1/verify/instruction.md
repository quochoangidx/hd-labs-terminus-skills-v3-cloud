Our account managers have been checking the period statements that the billing package in `/app/src/stockbill` produces for our warehouse clients against the client billing schedule, `/app/docs/billing-schedule.md` (WB-5), and several clients have queried their invoices with good reason. Pallets shipped out on the day a storage week begins escape that week, storage is billed on calendar weeks rather than on each lot's receipt anniversary, a lot that has aged into the long-stay tier has every week billed at that tier, a dispatch of forty pallets costs the same handling as a dispatch of one, movements on public holidays are billed at the weekday rate, weeks and movements from before the mid-June rate card were billed at the new rates, and a few discounts come out a cent short. The schedule is the authority for this statement. Change the package so that the statement printed by `python3 /app/tools/stockbill_run.py JOB.json` follows it. Each clause applies to every job inside the limits of clause 1.4, not just to jobs that look like the one in `/app/examples/`. We produce statements by running our own copy of `/app/tools/stockbill_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave the driver alone: the file you leave at that path must be byte-for-byte the one you were given.

Only change what the schedule governs. Where the schedule gives no rule for a value the package works out, that value keeps the calculation the package makes for it today, carried out on the values the schedule does define, and every other value still follows the schedule. Add no exception, clamp or guard of your own. Some jobs are left entirely open and the package may do whatever it likes with them: dates, counts, lengths, rates or percentages outside the limits of clause 1.4, a lot whose client is not in the job, two lots with the same id, dispatches that clause 1.4 does not allow, and files that do not follow the README.

Jobs of one to three hundred lots for one to twenty clients will be run. They include periods from a single day to sixty-two days, lots received before the period and during it, on every day of the week and on holidays, lots dispatched in one go or in up to fifty dispatches, on the day they arrived, on the first day of a storage week and on the last day of the period, lots still holding stock at the end of the period and lots never touched, stays long enough to reach every tier of a five-tier storage scale and stays that never leave the first, rate cards revised from one to six times with effective dates anywhere in the 400 days before the period ends, discounts from none to half on amounts that leave a half cent or more or less than one, and period ends from March 2001 to the end of 2099, around 29 February and the turn of a year. Every value in the statement is compared exactly, JSON type included, and so is the order of the lots.
