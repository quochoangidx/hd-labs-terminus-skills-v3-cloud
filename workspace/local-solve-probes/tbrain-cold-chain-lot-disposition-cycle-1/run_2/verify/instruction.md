Our QA release desk has been checking the lot disposition reports that the package in `/app/src/coldchain` produces against worksheets built from our cold-chain procedure, `/app/docs/qa-sop-lot-disposition.md` (QA-SOP-311), and the two do not agree. A lot that sat warm for a few hours on each of three trucks came out with most of its warm allowance left, a lot whose release certificate had already used up its hot allowance was released, a shipment held right on its labelled upper limit overnight was booked as a warm excursion, mean kinetic temperatures do not match the worksheet (one lot the worksheet put at 8.04 degrees against an 8.0 limit went out released), and loggers that skipped readings for three quarters of an hour never show up as unlogged time. The SOP is the authority for how our distribution chain disposes product lots. Change the package so that the report printed by `python3 /app/tools/coldchain_run.py JOB_DIR` follows it. Each of its rules holds for every job within the limits of its section 1, not only for jobs like the one in `/app/examples/`. Reports will be produced by running our own copy of `/app/tools/coldchain_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Leave alone whatever the SOP does not govern. Where the SOP names a figure but gives it no value for some input, the value the package's step works out for it today stands, worked from the figures the SOP does define, and every other step the lot goes through still keeps to the SOP; add no exception, clamp or guard of your own. Some jobs are left open, and whatever the package does with them is acceptable: values, stamps or counts outside the limits of section 1, bands that are not laid out the way 2.5 describes, a lot whose mean kinetic temperature falls inside the margins of 1.5, and job directories that do not follow the README.

Jobs of one to two hundred lots will be disposed. They include lots of one to twelve legs, one export serving several lots, handovers from the same minute to a week later, exports of two readings and of many thousands, logger spacing from one minute to a full day, readings on the labelled limits, on band ends and anywhere else section 1 allows, stability records with one to three bands on each side of the range, freeze points and activation ratios across their ranges, allowances of nought, release certificates with and without prior time, and remaining allowances that land exactly on nought. The `product`, `lot` and `disposition` values and the order of the lots are compared exactly, and so is every figure in `band_hours`, `remaining_hours`, `unlogged_hours` and `mkt_c`, which has to be a JSON number with a decimal point and the same value as the expected one.
