# Temporary Disability Benefits Manual (edition TD-7)

Claims Unit, State Industrial Commission.

This manual governs the temporary disability statements the claims unit sends
out with indemnity payments on accepted workers' compensation claims. Where the
statement package and this manual disagree, the manual is right. Rule numbers
stay the same from one edition to the next.

## 1. Units, rounding and limits

1.1 Money is kept in whole cents. When a rule multiplies or divides an amount
of money, the result is rounded to the nearest cent, and an exact half cent is
rounded up. A figure that no rule multiplies or divides is not rounded.

1.2 Days are calendar days, and a date is written YYYY-MM-DD. A payroll week
runs from a Monday through the Sunday after it and is known by that Sunday. A
benefit week is seven days.

1.3 A job gives the rate table and the claims to be stated. The table has 1 to
40 rows. Each row gives the date it takes effect, a weekly maximum of 20,000 to
400,000 cents and a weekly minimum of at least 1,000 cents and below that
maximum; each row takes effect later than the row above it. A job has 1 to 200
claims. Each has a claim number of 1 to 24 characters that no other claim in
the job shares, and a date of injury on or after the day the first row takes
effect. Every date in a job falls in the years 2016 to 2035.

1.4 A claim's wage lines are the employer's payments to the worker before and
around the injury. Each gives the day it was paid and its gross in cents, from
1 to 500,000. The employer's payroll runs on Fridays and pays a worker's
regular wages for one payroll week in a single line. Pay outside the payroll,
such as a bonus, holiday pay or a make-up of short pay, goes out in a line of
its own on the day it is approved, and that day is never a Friday. A claim has
0 to 120 wage lines, each paid no more than 400 days before the date of injury
and no more than 30 days after it, and the claim's base period has a week's pay
for at least one of its weeks.

1.5 A claim has 1 to 12 disability periods, the spells of total disability the
treating doctor certified. Each gives its first and last day; the last day is
not before the first and less than 400 days after it. The periods are listed in
date order, the first begins on or after the date of injury, and each later one
begins after the last day of the one before it.

1.6 A claim's partial earnings list 0 to 104 payroll weeks after the worker
went back to lighter work, each named by its Sunday and giving what the worker
earned in it, from 0 to 500,000 cents. The weeks are listed in date order, no
week appears twice, and each begins after the claim's last disability day.

## 2. Definitions

2.1 The week of injury is the payroll week that holds the date of injury.

2.2 A week's pay is the payroll's line for one payroll week's work. The payroll
pays for a week on the Friday that follows the week's Sunday.

2.3 The base period is the thirteen payroll weeks before the week of injury.

2.4 A full-time worker is one whose base period has a week's pay for ten or more
of its weeks.

2.5 The row in force on a day is the last row of the rate table that takes
effect on or before that day.

2.6 A claim's disability days are the calendar days its disability periods
cover, the first and the last day of each period included.

2.7 The waiting period is a claim's first three disability days, or all of its
disability days when it has fewer than three.

2.8 A week's wage loss is the average weekly wage less what the worker earned in
the week, or nought when the week's earnings reach the average weekly wage.

## 3. Average weekly wage and weekly rate

3.1 A week's pay counts toward the payroll week whose work it pays.

3.2 The base-period wages are the total of the wage lines that count toward the
weeks of the base period.

3.3 A full-time worker's average weekly wage is the base-period wages divided by
thirteen.

3.4 The weekly rate is two thirds of the average weekly wage, but no more than
the maximum and no less than the minimum of the row in force on the date of
injury. Some neighbouring states pay 60 per cent; this state pays two thirds.

3.5 When the average weekly wage is below that minimum, the weekly rate is the
average weekly wage itself.

## 4. Temporary total disability

4.1 The days of the waiting period are not paid unless the claim has fourteen
disability days or more. When it has, the days of the waiting period are paid
as retroactive days.

4.2 The paid days are the disability days outside the waiting period, together
with any retroactive days.

4.3 The total-disability amount is the weekly rate times the paid days, divided
by seven.

## 5. Temporary partial disability

5.1 Each week in a claim's partial earnings is a week of partial disability.
Its benefit is two thirds of the week's wage loss, but no more than the weekly
rate.

5.2 The partial amount is the total of the benefits of the weeks of partial
disability.

## 6. The statement

6.1 A claim's statement gives its average weekly wage, its weekly rate, the
number of days in its waiting period, its retroactive days, its paid days, its
total-disability amount, its number of weeks of partial disability, its partial
amount, and the total owed, which is the total-disability amount plus the
partial amount.
