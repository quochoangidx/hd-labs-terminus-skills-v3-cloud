Growers and their agents keep ringing the claims office because the crop-hail claim statements that the package in `/app/src/hailadj` draws up from our adjusters' inspection sheets disagree with what the office works out by hand from our Crop-Hail Loss Adjustment Procedure, `/app/docs/loss-adjustment-procedure.md` (edition 7). Fields with patchy damage come out far worse than their plots show, late-season leaf damage is priced wrongly, fields where hail both thinned the stand and stripped the leaves can be paid more than the crop was worth, deductibles rarely match the policy, cheques are often a cent short, and some small losses and small claims are still paid although the procedure holds them back. The procedure is the authority for these insurance claims. Fix the package so that `python3 /app/tools/hail_run.py JOB.json` prints statements that follow it; each rule of the procedure holds for every job inside the limits of its section 1, not only for jobs like the one in `/app/examples/`. We will draw up statements by running our own copy of that driver against your `/app/src`, and we compare the driver file you leave behind with ours byte for byte, so leave `/app/tools/hail_run.py` exactly as it is.

Carry each rule of the procedure exactly as far as its words go, and no further. Where the package works out a figure, or takes a step with a plot, a field or a claim, that no rule of the procedure reaches, it keeps the exact calculation today's code makes, with every constant of the package at its value today; of that calculation's inputs, only those the procedure itself sets may change. That comes before everything else in this paragraph, and everything else about the same claim still follows the procedure. Add no exception, clamp or guard of your own. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: figures, lengths and counts outside the limits of section 1, growth stages and deductible options the procedure does not list, claims or fields that break its uniqueness rules, and job files that do not follow the README.

The jobs we run hold one to fifty claims of one to thirty fields each, with fields of 1.0 to 5,000.0 acres insured for 10 to 2,000 dollars an acre, under every deductible option and at every growth stage on the chart. A field has one to forty sample plots with stands of 20 to 200 plants, from no plant lost to the whole row dead and from no leaf damage to complete defoliation, and replanting from none to the whole field. Losses fall on and around every limit the procedure sets, and claims fall on both sides of the minimum. Every value in a statement is compared exactly and has to be an integer, apart from the claim numbers and field codes, which are the strings given. Claims come in job order and fields in claim order, each with exactly the keys the README lists.
