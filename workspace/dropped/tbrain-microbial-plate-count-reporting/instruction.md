Our microbiology section has been checking the aerobic plate count certificates that the package in `/app/src/platecount` writes against hand calculations from the laboratory's procedure, `/app/docs/apc-sop.md` (SOP MIC-14), and they do not agree. A proficiency-test sample that the analysts spread at 0.1 mL came back ten times too low, a raw-milk batch whose plates were all too numerous to count went out as "less than", a sample with good plates at 10^-2 and at 10^-3 was reported from the 10^-2 plates alone, and a count that should read 1.3E3 was written 1.2E3. The SOP is the authority for these results. Change the package so that the report printed by `python3 /app/tools/platecount_run.py BATCH.json` follows it. Each of its rules holds for every batch within the limits of its section 1, not only for batches like the one in `/app/examples/`. Batches will be reported by running our own copy of `/app/tools/platecount_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the SOP governs. Where the SOP gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, keep the figure that step works out today, from the figures the SOP does define; every other step still follows the SOP. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the SOP does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: readings, steps, volumes or counts of samples, dilutions or plates outside the limits of section 1, a sample whose steps are listed twice or out of order, two samples with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Batches of one to two hundred samples will be reported. They include samples weighed in grams and measured in millilitres, one to eight dilutions at any steps from 0 to 9, series that plate every step and series that skip steps, dilutions plated with 1.0 mL and with 0.1 mL in one sample, one to four plates on a dilution, plates of every kind side by side on one dilution, readings from 0 to 5,000 colonies including 19, 20, 300 and 301, plates marked as too numerous to count, samples with countable plates at one dilution or at several, samples with no countable plate at all, and results that land exactly on a half at their second significant figure. The `batch`, `id`, `unit`, `kind` and `apc` values and the order of the samples are compared exactly.
