   Why this fails:
     Reach: Use
     {"survey":"CAP","workers":[{"id":"A","group":"G","shift_minutes":481,"log":[[481,115.0]],"peaks":[]}]}.
     Every field is within section 1. Reference duration is 480 / 2^10 = 0.46875 minutes.
     Sampled time equals the shift, so the required shift dose is 100 * 481 / 0.46875 =
     102613.33333333333, without projection or a cap.
     Flip: Take an otherwise correct repair and return min(correct_shift_dose, 102400.0) from
     shift_dose, leaving worker and group derivations intact. It returns 102400 for this worker
     and its singleton group, an error of 213.33333333333. The comparator's corresponding
     tolerance would be approximately 0.00000513067, so a full comparison of this distinguishing
     input would fail.
     The shown complete-report fixtures have no worker dose above 102400; reference_duration A7
     and ceiling_level A2 reach exactly 102400. Thus the clamp leaves every worker dose
     unchanged, and consequently leaves every derived TWA, status and group row unchanged.
     Runtime relabelling only changes labels/order and splits runs at the same level, preserving
     exposure totals. Differential cases also remain unchanged: above_ceiling pairs have ten
     capped-level minutes plus lower-level readings and doses below 3000; below_three_quarters
     pairs remain below 400; measured_nothing pairs remain zero. Their equality/zero assertions
     therefore still pass. The mutation does not alter the driver or protected state, so the
     integrity checks are unaffected.
     This is a reachable corner-cutting error: treating the dose at the continuous-level ceiling
     over the reference eight-hour day as a maximum exposure dose. It requires no knowledge of a
     private parsing or comparison quirk. Instruction.md explicitly prohibits adding an
