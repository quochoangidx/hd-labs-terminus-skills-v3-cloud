Our commercial office has been checking the demurrage statements that the billing package in `/app/src/demurrage` produces against the terminal's tariff, `/app/docs/demurrage-tariff.md` (DM-3), and they do not agree. Every container's dwell comes out a day short, reefers are given the free time of a dry box, and long stays are never charged the higher rate the tariff sets once a container is well into demurrage. The tariff is the authority for these statements. Change the package so that the statement printed by `python3 /app/tools/demurrage_run.py RELEASES.json` follows it. Each rule holds for every release file within the limits of rule 1.2, not only for files like the one in `/app/examples/`. Files will be billed by running our own copy of `/app/tools/demurrage_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the tariff governs. Where the tariff gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the tariff does define, and every other step still follows the tariff. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the tariff does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: types, days, rates, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.2, two containers with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Release files of one to two hundred containers will be billed. They include all three types, containers picked up on their discharge day, inside their free time, on its last day, just past it and up to 120 days later, demurrage of fewer than four days, exactly four and more, and daily rates from 1 cent to 100,000. Every key the README lists is compared exactly, as a JSON integer or string, and so is the order of the containers.
