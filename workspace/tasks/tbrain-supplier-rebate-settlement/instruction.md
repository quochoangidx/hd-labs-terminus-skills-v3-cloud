Our rebate desk has been redoing supplier settlements by hand from the agreement's rebate schedule, `/app/docs/rebate-schedule.md` (Schedule R, edition R-4), and the statements the package in `/app/src/rebate` draws up keep disagreeing with them. Freight still on the road at a quarter end lands in the wrong quarter, tiers and rebates rarely match the desk's worksheets, growth bonuses come out several times what the supplier expects to pay, return credits disagree with the supplier's credit notes, and some settlements the desk would hold back still go out. The schedule is the authority for these supplier settlements. Fix the package so that `python3 /app/tools/rebate_run.py JOB.json` prints statements that follow it; each rule of the schedule holds for every job inside the limits of its section 1, not only for jobs like the one in `/app/examples/`. We will draw up statements by running our own copy of that driver against your `/app/src`, and we compare the driver file you leave behind with ours byte for byte, so leave `/app/tools/rebate_run.py` exactly as it is.

Take each rule of the schedule as far as its words reach and stop there. A figure the package works out, or a step it takes with an item, that no rule of the schedule reaches keeps the exact calculation today's code makes, with every constant of the package at its value today; the only inputs of that calculation that change are those the schedule itself sets. This comes before everything else in this paragraph, and everything else about the same account follows the schedule. Add no exception, clamp or guard of your own. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: figures, dates, lengths and counts outside the limits of section 1, tier tables or accounts that break its ordering and uniqueness rules, and job files that do not follow the README.

The jobs we run hold one to a hundred accounts against tier tables of one to twelve rows, with thresholds up to 100,000,000,000 cents and rates of 0 to 1,500 basis points. They include quarters from 2020-Q1 to 2039-Q4; invoices, returns, price notices and sales on any day from 45 days before a quarter to 45 days after it, the first and last days of the quarter among them; ledgers with no lines at all and ledgers of 400 lines, from a single loose unit to 5,000 units at 100 to 1,000,000 cents a unit; returns heavy enough to take net purchases below nought; net purchases exactly on a threshold; last year's net purchases of nought, of one cent and of 100,000,000,000 cents; price notices from a one-cent tidy-up to cuts of nearly the whole price, on 100 to 50,000 units; sales from a cent under cost to far under it; and settlements on both sides of the minimum. Every value in a statement is compared exactly and has to be an integer, apart from the account code, which is the string given. The statements have to come in job order, each carrying exactly the keys the README lists.
