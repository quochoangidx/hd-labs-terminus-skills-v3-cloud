Our business billing team has been checking the data statements that the billing package in `/app/src/usagebill` produces against the carrier's tariff, `/app/docs/data-usage-tariff.md` (MD-2), and they do not agree. Usage is measured on the cycle's kilobytes added up instead of session by session, BASIC and PLUS lines are all given the same allowance, and heavy users are still charged their full overage rate on every megabyte instead of the tariff's lower rate past the first gigabyte of overage. The tariff is the authority for these statements. Change the package so that the statement printed by `python3 /app/tools/usagebill_run.py USAGE.json` follows it. Each rule holds for every account file within the limits of rule 1.2, not only for files like the one in `/app/examples/`. Files will be billed by running our own copy of `/app/tools/usagebill_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the tariff governs. Where the tariff gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the tariff does define, and every other step still follows the tariff. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the tariff does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: plans, sessions, rates, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.2, two lines with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Account files of one to two hundred lines will be billed. They include all three plans, lines with no sessions, sessions of one kilobyte and of whole and part megabytes, lines inside their allowance, exactly on it, just over it and far over it, overage of fewer than 1,024 megabytes, exactly 1,024 and more, and rates from 1 cent to 500. Every key the README lists is compared exactly, as a JSON integer or string, and so is the order of the lines.
