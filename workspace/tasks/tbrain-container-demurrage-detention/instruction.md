Our billing desk has been checking the demurrage and detention statements that the package in `/app/src/ddbill` produces against the carrier's tariff rules, `/app/docs/tariff-rules.md` (DT-3), and the consignees disputing them are right. Boxes are charged for weekends, port holidays and the storm closure that fell inside their terminal free time, every stay on the statement is a day shorter than the gate records show and detention starts a day late, a box that runs well past its free time is billed every day at its top tier, boxes whose charges began before the March rate revision were priced at the March rates, and a few discounts come out a cent short. The tariff rules are the authority for this statement. Change the package so that the statement printed by `python3 /app/tools/ddbill_run.py JOB.json` follows them. Each rule applies to every job inside the limits of rule 1.4, not just to jobs that look like the one in `/app/examples/`. We produce statements by running our own copy of `/app/tools/ddbill_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave the driver alone: the file you leave at that path must be byte-for-byte the one you were given.

Only change what the tariff rules govern. Where the rules give no rule for a value the package works out, that value keeps the calculation the package makes for it today, carried out on the values the rules do define, and every other value still follows the rules. Add no exception, clamp or guard of your own. Some jobs are left entirely open and the package may do whatever it likes with them: dates, counts, lengths, rates or percentages outside the limits of rule 1.4, a container whose contract is not in the job, two containers with the same id, a history that rule 1.4 does not allow, and files that do not follow the README.

Jobs of one to three hundred containers under one to twenty contracts will be run. They include boxes discharged, gated out and returned on every day of the week and on the same day, boxes still at the terminal and boxes still out with the merchant at the cut-off, contracts with no free days and with the most rule 1.4 allows, holidays and closures inside free time, after it and on weekends, stays long enough to reach every tier of a five-tier scale and stays that never leave the first, rate sheets revised from one to six times with effective dates anywhere in the 400 days before the cut-off, discounts from none to half on amounts that leave a half cent or more or less than one, and cut-off dates from March 2001 to the end of 2099, around 29 February and the turn of a year. Every value in the statement is compared exactly, JSON type included, and so is the order of the containers.
