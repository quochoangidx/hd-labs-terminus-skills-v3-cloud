# SOP MIC-14: Aerobic plate count results

Tarnwell Food Laboratory, microbiology section. Revision 5.

This procedure is how the laboratory turns the plate readings of an aerobic
plate count into the result it reports for each sample. Where the plate-count
package and this procedure disagree, the procedure governs. Section numbers do
not move between revisions.

The laboratory validated its own counting range and reporting on its own
plates. Where that differs from a published reference method, the section says
so, and the laboratory's figure is the one to use.

## 1 Units, rounding and limits

1.1 A reading is a whole number of colonies. A volume is in millilitres. A
sample weighed in grams has its result in colony-forming units per gram
(CFU/g), and a sample measured in millilitres has it in CFU/mL.

1.2 Every figure is worked out exactly from the readings, without rounding. A
result is rounded once, when it is reported, to two significant figures, an
exact half going up. It is written as its first significant digit, a point,
its second significant digit, the letter E and the power of ten, with a minus
sign where the power is below nought and no sign or leading zero otherwise:
25,000 is written 2.5E4, 3.25 is written 3.3E0 and 0.25 is written 2.5E-1.
Nothing else is rounded.

1.3 This procedure provides for batches within these limits, and each limit
includes its ends:

- a batch holds from 1 to 200 samples; a sample id is 1 to 12 characters, each
  a capital letter, a digit or a hyphen, and no two samples in a batch share
  one;
- a sample has from 1 to 8 dilutions, listed from the lowest step to the
  highest with no step listed twice, and a step is a whole number from 0 to 9;
- every plate of one dilution was plated with the same volume, either 1.0 mL or
  0.1 mL, and a dilution has from 1 to 4 plates;
- a plate's reading is from 0 to 5,000 colonies, or is marked as too numerous
  to count.

## 2 Terms

2.1 The laboratory dilutes tenfold. The dilution at step k is 10^-k of the
sample, step 0 being the sample itself, so each step is a tenth of the step
before it. A sample's dilutions are the steps the analyst plated.

2.2 A plate's plated amount is the amount of sample on it: the volume plated on
it multiplied by its dilution, in grams or millilitres of sample.

2.3 A plate's colonies are its reading. A plate marked as too numerous to count
has no reading.

2.4 A countable plate is a plate with from 20 to 300 colonies. (The laboratory
validated 20 to 300 colonies on its 90 mm plates; the reference method's 25 to
250 does not apply.)

2.5 A crowded plate is a plate with more than 300 colonies, or one marked as
too numerous to count.

2.6 A sparse plate is a plate with fewer than 20 colonies, none included. A
sparse sample is a sample every one of whose plates is sparse.

2.7 The counted dilutions of a sample that has a countable plate are the first
of its dilutions to hold a countable plate, and also the dilution a tenth of
that one, when the analyst plated it and it holds a countable plate too.

## 3 Plates in a result

3.1 A plate takes part in a result with its colonies and its plated amount, and
only the plates a rule of section 4 names take part in that rule.

3.2 The laboratory does not correct a reading for spreading growth, for a
broken plate or for a plate poured twice. The analyst records what was read.

## 4 Results

4.1 A sample with a countable plate is given a count: the colonies on the
countable plates of its counted dilutions, divided by the sum of those plates'
plated amounts. (The reference-method divisor with n1 + 0.1 n2 gives the same
figure only when both dilutions were plated with the same volume.)

4.2 A sparse sample with a colony on one of its plates is given an estimated
count: the colonies on the plates of the first of its dilutions to hold a
colony, divided by the sum of those plates' plated amounts.

4.3 A sparse sample with no colony on any of its plates is given a less-than
result: fewer than one colony in the plated amount of one plate of its first
dilution. The figure reported is one divided by that plated amount, written
after a < sign.

4.4 A sample every one of whose plates is crowded is given a greater-than
result: more than 300 colonies in the plated amount of one plate of its last
dilution. The figure reported is 300 divided by that plated amount, written
after a > sign.

## 5 The report

5.1 Each sample's result is reported with its kind: "count" for 4.1,
"estimate" for 4.2, "below" for 4.3 and "above" for 4.4.
