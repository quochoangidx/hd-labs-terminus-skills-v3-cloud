Our tire shop has been checking the fleet's tire compliance summaries by hand against its tread standard, `/app/docs/tread-standard.md` (edition TS-6), and the summaries the package in `/app/src/treadcheck` produces keep disagreeing with the shop's figures. Depths come out as the gauge showed them, whichever gauge was used; new tires are credited with far slower wear than the tread they have actually lost since they went on; tires stay in service past the depths the standard sets for their axle, and a tire sitting exactly on its limit is never pulled; and casings are sent for retread that are too old or have been retreaded too often, while the odd rate is a tenth off. The standard is the authority for these summaries. Fix the package so that `python3 /app/tools/treadcheck_run.py JOB.json` prints summaries that follow it; every rule of the standard holds for every job inside the limits of its section 1, not only for jobs like the one in `/app/examples/`. We will produce summaries by running our own copy of that driver against your `/app/src`, and we compare the driver file you leave behind with ours byte for byte, so leave `/app/tools/treadcheck_run.py` exactly as it is.

Change only what the standard governs. A figure in a summary, or any figure it is worked from, that no rule of the standard settles is worked out as today's code works it out, with the package's own constants at their present values; only those inputs to that working which the standard does settle take the standard's values. This comes before everything else in this paragraph, and everything else about the same tire follows the standard. Add no exception, clamp or guard of your own. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: figures, dates, lengths and counts outside the limits of section 1, readings that break its ordering rules, and job files that do not follow the README.

The jobs we run hold one to three hundred tires and one to twenty gauges. They include gauges with offsets from -9 to 9 tenths, nought among them; tires at every position, new depths from 60 to 320 tenths; logs of two to thirty readings over several years, readings down to 10 tenths, and tires whose latest depth sits on, just above or just below a limit; casings made on every day around their age limit and tires retreaded nought to four times; and odometers from nought to 5,000,000 km. Every value in a summary is compared exactly: `tire` and `position` are the strings given, `status` is one of the three strings the README lists, `retread` and `regroove` are booleans, and every other value has to be an integer. Entries come in job order, each carrying exactly the keys the README lists, and every job keeps within the limits of section 1.
