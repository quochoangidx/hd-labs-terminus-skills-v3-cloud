Our pharmacy quality analyst has been checking the adherence report that the measure package in `/app/src/pdcmeasure` produces from our pharmacy claims against hand calculations from the plan's specification, `/app/docs/adherence-measure-spec.md` (AM-2), and the two do not agree. Members who refill a few days early come out with gaps they never had, a member who filled a statin in January and stopped in March looks fully adherent for the year, a member who picked up two packs on one day is counted in the measure, a PDC of 79.96 is reported as 79.9, and the class sheet divides by members who were never in the measure. The specification is the authority for this report. Change the package so that the report printed by `python3 /app/tools/pdc_run.py CLAIMS.json` follows it. Each of its rules holds for every claims file within the limits of its rule 1.3, not only for files like the one in `/app/examples/`. Claims files will be measured by running our own copy of `/app/tools/pdc_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the specification governs. Where the specification gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the specification does define, and every other step still follows the specification. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the specification does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: dates, days supplies, codes, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.3, two members with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Claims files of one to four hundred members with fills under one to forty class codes will be measured, in measurement years from 2000 to 2099, leap years included. They include members with no fills and members with up to 400, fills on any day of the year from 1 January to 31 December, days supplies from 1 to 365 days, refills of one drug that overlap the fills before them by one day or by months, several drugs in one class, several fills on one day, first fills on every day of the year, stays of one day up to sixty stay days, classes of one member and of many, and PDCs and rates that land exactly on a half tenth or on 80.0. Every report key the README lists is compared exactly, and so is the order of both lists: `pdc` and `rate` have to be the reported tenth, and every other value has to be equal and of the same JSON type.
