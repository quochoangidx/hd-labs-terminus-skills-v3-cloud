Our operator's office has been checking the exit statements that the charging package in `/app/src/parkfee` produces against the car park's tariff, `/app/docs/garage-tariff.md` (GT-2), and they do not agree. Drivers of cars and vans who drop someone off and leave within a quarter of an hour are being charged, vans are paying the car rate, and a car left for a long weekend runs up a fee far past the tariff's daily maximum. The tariff is the authority for these statements. Change the package so that the statement printed by `python3 /app/tools/parkfee_run.py SESSIONS.json` follows it. Each rule holds for every exits file within the limits of rule 1.2, not only for files like the one in `/app/examples/`. Files will be billed by running our own copy of `/app/tools/parkfee_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the tariff governs. Where the tariff gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the tariff does define, and every other step still follows the tariff. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the tariff does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: vehicles, minutes, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.2, two sessions with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Exits files of one to two hundred sessions will be billed. They include all three vehicles, validated and unvalidated tickets, stays of 0, 15, 16, 60 and 61 minutes, stays that end exactly on a whole number of days and a minute past it, and stays of up to seven days. Every key the README lists is compared exactly, as a JSON integer or string, and so is the order of the sessions.
