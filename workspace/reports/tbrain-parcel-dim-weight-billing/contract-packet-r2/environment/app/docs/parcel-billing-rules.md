# Parcel Billing Rules PB-4

Halden Freight Parcel Services, billing office. Fourth edition.

These rules are how a day's shipped parcels are turned into the charges on the
shipper's invoice. Where the billing package and these rules disagree, the
rules govern. Rule numbers do not move between editions.

## 1 Units and limits

1.1 A side is a whole number of inches. An actual weight is given in tenths of
a pound, a whole number. Money is a whole number of cents.

1.2 Every figure is worked out from unrounded figures.

1.3 These rules provide for manifests within these limits, and each limit
includes its ends:

- a manifest lists from 1 to 300 parcels, and no two share an id; an id is 1 to
  12 characters, each a capital letter, a digit or a hyphen;
- each of a parcel's three sides is from 1 to 108 inches;
- an actual weight is from 1 to 1,500 tenths of a pound;
- a service is "GROUND" or "EXPRESS", a zone is a whole number from 2 to 8,
  and a parcel is either going to a home (residential) or not.

## 2 Weight

2.1 A box is a parcel each of whose three sides is 2 inches or more.

2.2 Every parcel has a dimensional weight, in pounds: its length times its
width times its height, in cubic inches, divided by its dimensional divisor.
The dimensional divisor of a box is 139.

2.3 A parcel's billable weight is the larger of its actual weight and its
dimensional weight, rounded up to the next whole pound. A weight that is
already a whole number of pounds stays as it is.

## 3 Charges

3.1 A parcel's transport charge is its billable weight in pounds times the
rate for its zone: 95 cents for zone 2, 105 for zone 3, 118 for zone 4, 131
for zone 5, 146 for zone 6, 164 for zone 7 and 190 for zone 8.

3.2 Every parcel going to a home carries a residential surcharge; a parcel
that is not going to a home carries none. The residential surcharge of a
ground parcel is 530 cents.

3.3 A parcel's fuel surcharge is 14.25 per cent of the sum of its transport
charge and its residential surcharge, rounded to the nearest cent, an exact half cent
going up.

3.4 A parcel's total is its transport charge, residential surcharge and fuel
surcharge added together. A manifest's total is the sum of its parcels'
totals.
