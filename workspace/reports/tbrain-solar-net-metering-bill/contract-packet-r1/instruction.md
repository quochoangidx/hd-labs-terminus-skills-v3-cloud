Our billing office has been checking the bills that the package in `/app/src/netbill` produces against the cooperative's net metering tariff, `/app/docs/net-metering-tariff.md` (NM-4), and they do not agree. Heavy home users are never charged the higher rate the tariff sets past their first 300 kilowatt-hours, shops are paying the home service charge, and meters that did nothing all cycle are still billed a service charge. The tariff is the authority for these bills. Change the package so that the bills printed by `python3 /app/tools/netbill_run.py METERS.json` follow it. Each rule holds for every meter reads file within the limits of rule 1.2, not only for files like the one in `/app/examples/`. Files will be billed by running our own copy of `/app/tools/netbill_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the tariff governs. Where the tariff gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the tariff does define, and every other step still follows the tariff. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the tariff does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: tariffs, kilowatt-hours, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.2, two meters with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Meter reads files of one to two hundred meters will be billed. They include all three tariffs, meters that imported more than they exported, the same, and less, home nets of 299, 300, 301 kilowatt-hours and far more, and meters that neither imported nor exported or did only one of the two. Every key the README lists is compared exactly, as a JSON integer or string, and so is the order of the bills.
