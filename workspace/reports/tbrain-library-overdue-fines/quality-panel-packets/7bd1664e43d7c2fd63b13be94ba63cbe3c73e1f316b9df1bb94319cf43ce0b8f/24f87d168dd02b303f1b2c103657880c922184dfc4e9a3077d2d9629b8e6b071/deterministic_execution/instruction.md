Our circulation office has been checking the fines statements that the package in `/app/src/finebook` produces against the county's overdue fines policy, `/app/docs/overdue-fines-policy.md` (LF-5), and they do not agree. Books and DVDs are all given the same loan period, borrowers are being fined for Sundays when every branch is shut, and a long-lost DVD can run up a fine many times what it would cost to replace. The policy is the authority for these statements. Change the package so that the statement printed by `python3 /app/tools/finebook_run.py LOANS.json` follows it. Each rule holds for every return file within the limits of rule 1.2, not only for files like the one in `/app/examples/`. Files will be worked out by running our own copy of `/app/tools/finebook_run.py` against your `/app/src`, so leave that driver exactly as it is; the file you leave there has to match ours byte for byte.

Only change what the policy governs. Where the policy gives no rule for a figure that one of the package's steps works out, that step keeps working the figure out the way it does today, from the figures the policy does define, and every other step still follows the policy. Add no exception, clamp or guard that the policy does not give. Some inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them: kinds, days, fines, costs, counts or lengths outside the limits of rule 1.2, two loans with the same id, and files that do not follow the README.

Return files of one to two hundred loans will be worked out. They include all three kinds, loans returned on the day they were borrowed, before their due day, on it, a day after it and up to 180 days after borrowing, due days and return days that fall on Sundays and late spans that cross one Sunday or many, fines that stop short of the replacement cost, reach it exactly and would pass it, and daily fines and replacement costs across their whole ranges. Every key the README lists is compared exactly, as a JSON integer or string, and so is the order of the loans.
