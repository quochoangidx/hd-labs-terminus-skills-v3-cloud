# Field Sales Compensation Plan (issue 6)

This plan sets how the sales-operations desk works out the quarterly commission
statement of each field sales rep. Where the statement package and this plan
disagree, the plan is right. Rule numbers stay the same from one issue to the
next.

## 1. Units, rounding and limits

1.1 Money is kept in whole cents. When a rule takes a share of an amount, at a
rate in basis points or at a split in per cent, the result is rounded to the
nearest cent, and an exact half cent goes up. A figure no rule takes a share of
is not rounded.

1.2 A date is written YYYY-MM-DD and days are calendar days. The quarters of a
year run January to March, April to June, July to September and October to
December; a quarter is written YYYY-Qn, as in 2031-Q2. A quarter includes its
first and its last day.

1.3 A job gives the reps whose statements are to be drawn up: 1 to 60 reps. Each
rep has a rep code of 1 to 16 characters that no other rep in the job shares,
the quarter being paid, in the years 2020 to 2039, and a quota of 100,000 to
10,000,000,000 cents. Each rep also has a draw of 0 to 10,000,000 cents, an
owed balance brought forward of 0 to 100,000,000 cents and a carried amount
brought forward of 0 to 24,999 cents.

1.4 A rep's orders are the CRM export's order records for the rep: 0 to 300
orders, each giving the day it was booked, the order's value of 100 to
1,000,000,000 cents, the rep's split of it, 1 to 100 per cent, and whether it
is the customer's first order. A customer's first order is often a trial order:
a few units bought to try the product before any real commitment.

1.5 A rep's credit notes are the credit notes billing raised against the rep's
orders: 0 to 100 credit notes, each giving the day it was raised, the day its
order was booked (the same day or up to 365 days earlier), the amount credited,
100 to 1,000,000,000 cents, and the rep's split of the order, 1 to 100 per
cent. Most credit notes cancel an order or take goods back; billing also raises
courtesy credits of a few dollars to a few hundred dollars for a late delivery
or a price keyed wrong on an invoice.

1.6 Every order a rep books and every credit note raised against the rep's
orders falls between 45 days before the first day of the rep's quarter and 45
days after its last day.

## 2. Definitions

2.1 A rep's quarter is the quarter the job names for the rep.

2.2 A rep's share of an amount is the rep's split, in per cent, of that amount.

2.3 A reversal is a credit note of 50,000 cents or more.

2.4 A new-logo order is a customer's first order of 250,000 cents or more.

2.5 The age of a credit note is the number of days from the day its order was
booked to the day it was raised.

## 3. Bookings

3.1 An order counts toward the quarter in which it was booked.

3.2 A rep's bookings are the total of the rep's shares of the orders that count
toward the rep's quarter.

## 4. Commission

4.1 Commission is earned on bookings in three bands. The part of the bookings
up to the quota earns 800 basis points. The part above the quota, up to twice
the quota, earns 1,200 basis points. The part above twice the quota earns
1,600 basis points.

4.2 The commission of each band is rounded on its own, and a rep's commission
is the total of the three.

## 5. Clawback

5.1 A reversal raised in the rep's quarter at an age of 120 days or less claws
back 800 basis points of the rep's share of it. A reversal raised in the rep's
quarter at a greater age claws back nothing.

5.2 Every credit note raised in the rep's quarter carries one clawback line,
and a rep's clawback is the total of its clawback lines.

## 6. New-logo bonus

6.1 A new-logo order that counts toward the rep's quarter earns a bonus of 300
basis points of the rep's share of it.

6.2 Every first order that counts toward the rep's quarter carries one bonus
line, and a rep's bonus is the total of its bonus lines. An order that is not a
customer's first order carries no bonus line.

## 7. Draw and payment

7.1 A rep's total is the commission, the bonus and the carried amount brought
forward, less the clawback.

7.2 When the total is less than the rep's draw, the rep is paid the draw,
nothing is recovered or carried, and the draw less the total is added to the
owed balance.

7.3 Otherwise the owed balance is recovered from the part of the total above
the draw, as far as that part goes, and the rep's due is the total less what
was recovered. What was recovered comes off the owed balance.

7.4 A rep whose draw is above nought is paid the due. A rep whose draw is
nought is paid a due of 25,000 cents or more in full; a smaller due is not paid
and is carried to the rep's next quarter. Nothing else is carried.
