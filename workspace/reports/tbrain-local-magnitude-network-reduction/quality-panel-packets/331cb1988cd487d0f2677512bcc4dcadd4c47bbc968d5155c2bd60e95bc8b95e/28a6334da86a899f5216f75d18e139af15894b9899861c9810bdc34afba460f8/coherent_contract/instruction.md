Our bulletin desk turns each located earthquake's amplitude picks into station and network local magnitudes with the package in `/app/mlnet`, and the analysts who spot-check it against part 4 of the network's processing manual, `/app/manual/npm4-local-magnitude.md` (NPM-4), keep finding disagreements: magnitudes come out a few tenths high across the board, stations close to a deep event are corrected as if it were at the surface, one station that reads well off the others can drag an event's magnitude a long way, and events that only two stations recorded still get a network value. NPM-4 is the authority for this work. Change the package so that the report printed by `python3 /app/tools/ml_bulletin.py BULLETIN.json` is the one NPM-4 describes, and read each of its rules as holding for every bulletin inside the limits of its section 1, not only for days like the one in `/app/samples/`. The driver `/app/tools/ml_bulletin.py` is not yours to change: bulletins are reduced by running our own copy of it against your `/app/mlnet`, and the `ml_bulletin.py` you leave there has to be identical to ours.

Only put right what NPM-4 actually governs. Where it gives no rule for a value, that value is still computed the way the code computes it today, fed with the quantities NPM-4 does define; add no exclusion, clamp or guard of your own for it. For such a value, keep the present calculation; every other step it passes through still follows NPM-4. A few inputs are left entirely open, and the package may do whatever it likes with them, whether they arrive through the driver or through a direct call: any figure outside the limits of section 1, and any file that breaks one of that section's other rules.

The bulletins we reduce hold one to fifty events and a station table of one to two hundred stations. They include events at every depth from the surface down to 60 km, recordings from a station sitting on the epicentre out to 1000 km away, stations with one to four horizontal channels, sites whose broadband and strong-motion sensors arrive as separate recordings, channels of every noise level, station corrections that are positive, zero and negative, and events with one, two, three or many contributing stations, in both odd and even numbers. Every bulletin keeps within the limits NPM-4 gives in section 1. In the report, every `id`, `code` and `contributes`, the order of every list, and whether an `ml` is null are compared exactly; every other number must agree with the expected one to within 10^-6.
